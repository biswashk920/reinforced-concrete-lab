How to use this app
  1. Pick a mode at the top: Beam design, Column design or Foundation design. In Foundation mode choose the footing type (isolated under axial load, isolated with a moment, sloped or stepped isolated, wall strip, combined two-column, or pile cap); the app sizes the plan from the soil bearing capacity, then finds the depth, bars, column bars and checks.
  2. Enter loads and size. Loads are characteristic (unfactored); the app applies the 1.5 factor. Beam: span in m, UDL in kN/m, optional point load at midspan or tip. Column: axial load, moments about both axes, unsupported lengths and the end condition.
  3. Choose materials. Concrete grade (fck) and steel grade (fy). Switch Seismic detailing (IS 13920) on for closer stirrups or ties in the end zones.
  4. Press Design beam or Design column. A green message means every check passed. Red rows in the check table show what fails and by how much.
  5. If a design fails, enlarge the section, raise the concrete grade or reduce the load. For beams, Find cheapest section lists the lowest-cost sizes that pass; press Use to apply one.
  6. Read the results. Provide gives bars, stirrup zones and spacing. Checks list each clause. Working shows intermediate values.
  7. Explore the 3D view. Drag to rotate, scroll or pinch to zoom, right-drag or Shift-drag to pan, arrow keys to rotate. Hover a bar or stirrup for its label. Use the layer boxes and opacity slider to see through the concrete, the preset buttons for standard views, and Play build to watch the cage assemble.
  8. Pinch (touch) or scroll (mouse) to zoom. Move the section slider to slide a cutting plane along the member and read the shear, moment and stirrup spacing at that point.
  9. Download. Download CSV saves inputs, results, checks, stirrup zones and a bar schedule. Save PNG saves the current 3D view. Print report prints the page.

Good to know: try the example buttons above the inputs to see stirrup zones, deep-beam side bars and seismic detailing. Simply supported beams get bar curtailment and an anchorage check; schedules use straight lengths without laps or bends. Continuous beams use approximate coefficients. This is an educational tool, not a validated design package. Have a qualified engineer verify any real design against the code.
